== Current status (2026-04-29) ==

Three reachability axioms (R1, R2, R3-narrow) were originally posited to bridge transient shapes that the macro rule set cannot directly handle. Of the three, '''R2''' (<code>M0(a::L', [r+3, 1, 2])</code>) and '''R3-narrow''' (<code>M0(a::L', (r+3) :: e :: f :: rest ++ [2])</code>) have been closed via Option 1 (<code>multi_bounce</code> + <code>shift_to_macro_prog</code>). <code>#print axioms Sweeper.sweeper_never_halts</code> shows the only remaining custom axiom is '''R1''' (<code>reach_M_nil_3</code>: <code>M([], 3, d :: R)</code>).

Empirical status of R1: the F1+F2 macro-step simulator (now ported to Rust as <code>era-sim</code>) records '''zero R1 firings''' across 99 G macro steps ≈ 6.2 × 10<sup>16</sup> raw TM steps (era 63,765). Every era boundary lies in the strict-canonical shape <code>M(L, c, [1])</code> with <code>|L| ≥ 1</code> and <code>c ≥ 4</code> — both conditions empirically incompatible with R1's shape <code>M([], 3, R)</code>. The remaining work is to lift this empirical fact into a structural-exclusion invariant (form A.3 of <code>invariant_strategy.md</code>: "reachable ∧ <code>c = 3</code> → <code>L ≠ []</code>").

== Failed proof strategies ==

The core difficulty is that the naive invariant <code>AllGe1 L ∧ c ≥ 1 ∧ AllGe1 R</code> is '''not preserved by non-halting''': many run-length configurations satisfying it actually reach the undefined <code>C,1</code> transition and halt. For example <code>M([], 3, R)</code> halts for any <code>R</code>, as do <code>M([], 7, R)</code>, <code>M([], 15, R)</code>, etc. The "bad" cursor values at <code>L = []</code> include <code>{3, 7, 15, 31, 63, …}</code> plus a Collatz-like cascade of even values reached through them — while the reachable orbit in fact avoids all of these. Several attempts to characterize the safe set failed:

* '''Step-level invariants (C1Inv / SafeRight).''' Tracking a step-by-step predicate on the raw TM tape turned out to be unworkable: the bookkeeping for each phase transition explodes. Abandoned in favour of macro-step reasoning.

* '''Mersenne-exclusion invariant.''' Adding <code>L = [] → ¬IsMersenne c</code> (i.e. <code>c ∉ {3,7,15,31,…}</code>) to the invariant. This was preserved by most rules but cascaded into conditions on <code>L.head</code> at <code>c=1</code>, which cascaded again into <code>c=7</code>, and so on — an '''infinite''' chain of increasingly complicated per-field conditions. Removed.

* '''Compound-transition strengthening (Approach C).''' Proving chained macro rules (<code>sweep_and_shift</code>, <code>zero_bounce_and_shift</code>, <code>era_and_sweep</code>) to try to skip over the dangerous transient shapes. Blocked because halting depends on the full triple <code>(L, c, R)</code>, not any single field: <code>M([], 5, [1])</code> halts but <code>M([], 11, [1])</code> does not, <code>M([], 7, R)</code> halts for every <code>R</code> but <code>M([], 4, [4,6,2])</code> does not. No simple per-field condition can separate the two.

* '''Strengthened EraPlusSweep predicate (<code>RTailOkay</code>).''' Restricting progress to configurations whose right-side run list "ends in 1 whenever it has ≥ 2 elements". Preservation fails at <code>sweep_and_shift</code> on <code>M(L, 3, [1])</code>, which produces <code>M(…, [1, 2])</code> — a multi-element right side ending in 2, precisely the shape the predicate was meant to exclude.

* '''macroEra function refinement.''' Defining a recursive <code>macroEra</code> that chains rules automatically and proves progress by computation. This closed most cases but left the same three transient shapes uncovered; they are genuinely reached by the raw TM and have no direct macro rule.

* '''Backward cascade for R1 (Phase 2 layered analysis).''' Characterizing macroStep predecessors of <code>M([], 3, R)</code> layer by layer. Layers 0–4 completed (<code>phase2.lean</code>, ≈ 64 axiom-clean lemmas), but Layer 5+ branches unboundedly: each new producer shape begets two or more deeper producer shapes, with no fixed point in sight. Per-field invariants over the resulting branch tree are also ruled out (5 prior strengthening attempts failed for related reasons). Suspended; the realistic continuation is forward-simulation-driven derivation rather than backward enumeration.

* '''Empirical era-state recurrence search.''' A native-code era simulator (<code>era-sim</code>, Rust port of <code>macro_sim.py</code>, bit-identically cross-validated) generated 63,765 era-boundary states, all of the canonical form <code>M(L, c, [1])</code>. Numerical experiments looked for a low-order recurrence on era state — linear fits on <code>L_head[n+1]</code>, ratio statistics, residue patterns mod 4, periodicity, fixed-point attractors. All uniformly negative: <code>L_head[n+1] / L_head[n]</code> ranges over 0.006 … 378 with no clustering; <code>L_head, c, L_sum mod 4</code> are uniformly distributed; all 63,765 <code>(L, c)</code> configs are distinct. Rules out forms C.2 (era recurrence) and C.4 (era-modular invariant) from <code>invariant_strategy.md</code> at the level of low-order arithmetic on era state. (The 2-adic-string analogue — a recurrence on <code>L</code> as a 2-adic digit sequence rather than on <code>L_head</code> alone — has not been ruled out.)

* '''R3 doubling-chain decomposition (informational only).''' Of the 6,478 R3 firings recorded by the simulator, 29 % satisfy <code>R[0] = L[0] + 1</code>, and 1 % exhibit a clean recursive pattern <code>R[k] = 2<sup>k−1</sup>·(2a + 1)</code> for <code>1 ≤ k ≤ chain_len</code>, with maximum chain length 11. This is the within-bridge structure of an R3 firing and was not needed for the Lean proof — the existing <code>multi_bounce_general</code>/<code>shift_to_macro_prog</code> closure handles all R3-narrow shapes uniformly without parametrizing the doubling. Listed here for completeness because it consumed analysis time before being recognized as not load-bearing.
